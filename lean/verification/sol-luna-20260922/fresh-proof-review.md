# Independent audit: the fixed-field conditional sequence theorem

**Scope.** I derived the statement and scalar bridge from the current
`ChallengeZeta.lean`, `SolutionZeta.lean`, and their named theorem sources,
then checked the core target and the corresponding manuscript conventions.
I did not compile Lean or replay the proof. This is a mathematical audit of
the implication and its displayed definitions, not a proof of its numerical
hypothesis and not a fresh line-by-line audit of every imported theorem.

## Finding

I found no concrete mismatch in the claim that the one displayed fixed-field
inequality implies the exact sequence target, assuming the imported bridge
theorems are correctly proved. In particular, the exponent, plane, pair
normalization, and sequence quantifiers agree with the target. The result is
conditional: the numerical hypothesis remains unproved in Lean, so this does
not establish the unconditional planar theorem.

## Independent normalization and exponent check

The challenge defines
\[
 \operatorname{unitPairs}(U)=\frac{1}{2}
 \#\{(x,y)\in U^2:|x-y|=1\},\qquad
 \alpha=\frac{2083647}{2000000}.
\]
Here `dist` is the standard complex Euclidean distance. Since distance one
excludes the diagonal and is symmetric, every unordered edge has exactly two
ordered representatives. The manuscript explicitly says its construction
counts ordered edges and passes to unordered edges by dividing by two.
Independently, `UnitDistance.Counting.ordered_card_eq_twice_unordered` proves
the exact cardinality identity, and `unitPairs_eq_unordered_card` identifies
the real-valued definition with the direct `Sym2` count. Thus the factor of
two is the ordinary unordered-pair normalization, not a change to the graph
or metric.

The core arithmetic exponent is `1 + increment`, where
\[
 \delta=\texttt{increment}=\frac{83647}{2000000}=0.0418235,
 \qquad 1+\delta=\frac{2083647}{2000000}=1.0418235.
\]
`Target.exponent_eq` proves this rational identity exactly. The selected
finite-graph estimates use `unitPairs(U)/(U.card)^(1+increment)`, so no
rounding or exponent conversion is needed at the endpoint.

## Independent scalar and quantifier trace

Write `K` for the explicitly generated degree-524288 field,
\(\delta_0=1/12000\), and
\(\ell=(9/4)\log 2+(1/2)\log 15015\). The displayed premise is exactly
\[
 X_K:=\frac{\log(\operatorname{Re}\zeta_K(1+\delta_0))}{524288}
 +\delta_0\left(
   \frac{\ell-\gamma-\log(4\pi)}4
   -\frac{\operatorname{Re}(\zeta'_K(2)/\zeta_K(2))}{524288}
 \right)
 < \frac{42165819}{10^9}=0.042165819.
\]
The Lean statement uses `logDeriv (dedekindZeta K) 2`, the ordinary
logarithmic derivative. There is one real inequality argument; the other
numbers and functions are defined in the statement/imports, not additional
premises.

The bridge theorem rewrites the fixed-base residue scalar as this zeta and
log-derivative expression, rewrites the degree to 524288, and transports the
values from the independently generated field to the arithmetic retained
field by a rational algebra equivalence. Its terminal ceiling is
`ceiling + 4/10^6 = 0.042165819`; this is why the newer premise is four
millionths weaker than the earlier `0.042161819` checkpoint. The scalar
equality and strict inequality are consistent; no extra degree factor or
relative-degree factor appears in this rewriting.

The downstream strictness is used rather than discarded. If \(X_K<T\),
where \(T=0.042165819\), the proof takes the midpoint
\(U=(X_K+T)/2\), giving \(X_K<U<T\). Fixed-base inheritance yields an
eventual normalized relative-residue bound at most `U`. The sharper-pair
margin and the root-discriminant/signature bounds then give a fixed positive
lower bound \(\eta=T-U>0\) for the arithmetic rate. For all sufficiently
large family indices, the finite construction supplies a finite
\(U_j\subset\mathbb C\) with
\[
 \frac{\exp(d_j\eta)}{2^{3+\delta}}
 \le \frac{\operatorname{unitPairs}(U_j)}{|U_j|^{1+\delta}},
 \qquad d_j\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
Consequently the normalized pair count tends to infinity. The sequence
extraction theorem chooses witnesses at the eventual indices (and arbitrary
empty sets at earlier indices). Its counting bound, together with
\(1+\delta<2\), forces the chosen cardinalities to tend to infinity as
well. This is a sequence indexed by natural numbers; it makes no claim for
every sufficiently large cardinality.

## Field and scope checks

The challenge field is generated by the seven stated rational square roots
and twelve stated retained roots in an algebraic closure. The source proves
that the independently specified field is algebraically equivalent to the
arithmetic retained field and has degree 524288. The choices of square-root
signs do not introduce a visible mismatch in the claimed zeta quantity:
the proof transports through that field equivalence, and the ordinary
Dedekind zeta and its logarithmic derivative are invariant under the
equivalence.

The sets are `Finset ℂ`, with ordinary `dist`; there is no altered metric,
multiplicity, or assumption of witnesses for each cardinality. The exact
target uses both `card(U_j) → ∞` and the normalized unordered-pair ratio
`→ ∞`, and the selected implication has that same shape.

## Independent audit of the Euler comparison and five-allowance bound

I derived the local comparisons, Artin weights, restoration identity, and
rational allowance sum. I found no multiplicity, degree-normalization,
restoration, or sign-direction counterexample. This confirms the displayed
formulas subject to the stated field and local-index identifications. It is
not a fresh replay of the Hecke-factor enclosures or prime census.

### Local monotonicity and signed savings

For a rational prime \(p\) in a Galois field of degree \(n\), with local
indices \(e,f\), there are \(n/(ef)\) primes above \(p\), each of norm
\(p^f\). Its contribution to \(n^{-1}\log\zeta(s)\) is
\[
 \Phi_{p,s}(e,f)=\frac{-\log(1-p^{-fs})}{ef}.
\]
For \(s>1\), this is positive and decreases when either index increases:
increasing \(e\) increases the denominator, while increasing \(f\) both
increases the denominator and decreases \(-\log(1-p^{-fs})\). Local indices
multiply in a field tower. This proves the manuscript's outside-\(S'\)
upper comparison from \(N\) to \(M\), and from \(M\) to any retained
overfield. The formal \(\texttt{primeFiberEulerDefect}\) has the same sign:
it is degree times the base Euler-log contribution minus the sum of actual
terms above the base prime, and the formal theorem subtracts certified
finite defects from the base upper bound.

At the seven removed primes \(S'=\{2,3,5,7,11,13,17\}\), the manuscript
restores the actual \(M\)-factors. Their denominators use the stated local
indices: \((e,f)=(8,4)\) at 2, \((2,2)\) at 3 and 5, \((2,4)\) at 7,
11, and 13, and \((1,4)\) at 17. Thus
\[
 B_D(\sigma)=-\frac{\log(1-2^{-4\sigma})}{32}
 -\sum_{p=3,5}\frac{\log(1-p^{-2\sigma})}{4}
 -\sum_{p=7,11,13}\frac{\log(1-p^{-4\sigma})}{8}
 -\frac{\log(1-17^{-4\sigma})}{4}.
\]
This uses \(M\)'s actual indices, not \(N\)'s. At larger fields the normalized
local contributions can only decrease, so restoring \(M\)'s terms is an
upper bound there too.

Both finite corrections are lower bounds for positive local defects and
therefore are subtracted. For \(p\in\mathcal P_4\), outside \(S'\), the
stated tests give \(f_N(p)=2\), \(4\mid f_M(p)\), and \(e=1\). With
\(x=p^{-\sigma}\in(0,1)\), the defect is at least
\[
 \frac{-\log(1-x^2)}2-\frac{-\log(1-x^4)}4
 =\frac14\log\frac{1+x^2}{1-x^2}>0,
\]
the summand in \(S_4\). On the central support, \(p\) splits in \(N\)
(\(f_N=1\)) and is nonsplit in the full retained central field
(\(f_M\ge2\) even), again with \(e=1\). Its defect is at least
\[
 -\log(1-x)-\frac{-\log(1-x^2)}2
 =\frac12\log\frac{1+x}{1-x}=\operatorname{atanh}(x)>0,
\]
the \(S_{\rm cen}\) summand. The zero genus-vector condition makes the
central support disjoint from \(\mathcal P_4\). Hence the comparison has
the claimed direction:
\[
 \frac{\log\zeta_M(\sigma)}{[M:\mathbb Q]}
 \le Y(\sigma)+B_D(\sigma)-S_4-S_{\rm cen}.
\]

### Artin multiplicities and the \(Y\) normalization

For the degree-16384 Galois field \(N\), the manuscript lists 128
degree-one, 256 degree-two, 456 degree-four, and 124 degree-eight
irreducibles. The regular representation gives
\[
 \zeta_N^{S'}(s)=\prod_\rho L^{S'}(s,\rho)^{\dim\rho},
 \qquad
 128+256\cdot4+456\cdot16+124\cdot64=16384.
\]
The table's rows are sums of individual \(\log|L^{S'}(s,\rho)|\) values,
with both members of each conjugate pair included. It splits the 456
quartics into 448 mixed and 8 pure quartics. Thus one multiplies each row
sum by the representation degree once; the number of factors is already
accounted for inside the row sum. With row endpoints \(A_i\), the bound is
\[
 Y(\sigma)\le
 \frac{A_1+2A_2+4A_{4,\mathrm{mixed}}+4A_{4,\mathrm{pure}}+8A_8}{16384},
\]
where the endpoint combination is
\[
 \frac{7.337578130846530221
 +2(0.400886873183882259)
 +4(-0.046453348154273950)
 +4(0.058145052434373409)
 +8(-0.111176963043772737)}{16384}
 =0.00044535540710354679437255859375.
\]
It is below the formal allowance \(F=0.000445355407103547\).
The negative quartic and octic row endpoints remain valid upper bounds;
their positive weights do not change the inequality direction.

The main vulnerable bridge is the identification of this Artin product and
its analytic factors with the actual completed-field zeta. The manuscript
states the regular-representation identity and actual Hecke-factor
identifications. The formal local-to-global Artin theorem has the matching
shape, but requires primewise regular-factor identities and genuine Euler
products for the named factors; the generic theorem does not provide these
data by itself. The factor bounds and their field identifications remain
external checked inputs. I found no normalization counterexample but did
not reconstruct every Hecke representation or numeric factor enclosure.

### Exceptional restoration and five allowances

Set \(\sigma=12001/12000=1+\delta_0\), \(\delta_0=1/12000\), and
\(n_M=2^{19}=524288\). The Artin product is imprimitive at \(S'\).
Formally, the completed-field bound first deletes its \(S'\) contribution
\(C_D\):
\[
 \frac{\log\zeta_{\mathrm{Completed}}(\sigma)}{16384}-C_D\le F.
\]
The normalized defects over \(S'\) are exactly \(C_D-B_D\), where \(B_D\)
is the contribution of the retained field \(M\). The disjoint census
supports contribute at least \(S_4+S_{\rm cen}\). Dividing all defects by
\(n_M\), then subtracting them from the completed-field full zeta bound,
cancels \(C_D\):
\[
 \frac{\log\zeta_M(\sigma)}{n_M}
 \le F+B_D-S_4-S_{\rm cen}.
\]
This is the formal imprimitive replacement identity; it avoids counting
restored factors twice and handles differing local indices at \(S'\) with
the correct sign.

At \(s=2\), termwise differentiation of the absolutely convergent Euler
logarithm gives
\[
 -\frac{1}{n_M}\frac{\zeta'_M}{\zeta_M}(2)
 =\sum_p\frac{\log p}{e_M(p)(p^{2f_M(p)}-1)}>0.
\]
Each summand decreases with \(e_M,f_M\), so lower index floors give an upper
bound \(R\) for this positive debit. The formal identity
\(\texttt{normalizedPrimeDebit}=-\operatorname{Re}(\texttt{logDeriv})/n_M\)
shows that this has the same sign as the \(-\operatorname{Re}(\log\zeta_M)'(2)\)
term in \(hfinite\). Therefore the debit allowance is added.

The five directed allowances are
\[
\begin{array}{rl}
 F&=0.000445355407103547,\\
 B&=0.041727023150898786,\\
 S_4&=0.00003724741189544274807446\quad\text{(lower bound)},\\
 S_{\rm cen}&=0.00004201663320373563276702\quad\text{(lower bound)},\\
 \delta_0L&=0.00006870442657779491222606\quad\text{(upper bound)}.
\end{array}
\]
Their directions yield
\[
 H_M\le F+B-S_4-S_{\rm cen}+\delta_0L
 =0.04216181893948094953138458
 <0.042161819<0.042165819.
\]
The first strict comparison is exact rational arithmetic in
\(\texttt{allowance\_sum\_lt\_ceiling}\). Finally,
\[
 H_M=\frac{\log(\operatorname{Re}\zeta_M(\sigma))}{n_M}
 +\delta_0\left(\frac{\ell-\gamma-\log(4\pi)}4
 -\frac{\operatorname{Re}(\log\zeta_M)'(2)}{n_M}\right)
\]
is exactly the formal fixed-base scalar by the proved logarithmic-derivative
identity. It uses the same root-discriminant constant \(\ell\) and absolute
degree 524288; there is no missing relative-degree factor of two. The
manuscript's bound is stronger than the selected challenge hypothesis.

The unverified links are factual and analytic rather than an algebraic sign
mismatch: the field \(N\) in the Hecke data must be the specified completed
degree-16384 field; the table must bound those actual \(L\)-values; the
seven restored factors and both census supports must have the claimed local
indices; and the prime list, sieve completeness, and outward enclosures must
be correct. The conditional Lean theorem assumes the final scalar inequality
and does not discharge these links. This audit checked the formulas and
normalizations, not all underlying field identifications or numerical data.
