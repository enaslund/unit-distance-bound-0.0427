\begin{abstract}
Let $u(U)$ count the unordered pairs at distance one in a finite planar
set $U$. We construct finite sets $U_j$ with $|U_j|\to\infty$ and
$u(U_j)/|U_j|^{1.04273}\to\infty$. The largest exponent previously
claimed, $1.0418235$, is in the author's unpublished manuscript. The
method is the number-field construction of OpenAI and Sawin, in the form
developed there: unit distances come from elements of relative norm one
in quadratic extensions of mixed signature, and the fields come from an
infinite pro-$2$ class field tower. The new ingredient is a tower over
the real quadratic field $\Q(\sqrt{241})$, in which $2$, $3$ and $5$
split. Its Golod--Shafarevich polynomial contains two copies of the local
conditions at these primes but only one constant term, and the extra room
lets the tower avoid ramification at $7$, $11$ and $13$. The root
discriminant of the fields falls from about $583$ to about $286$. The
relative zeta value is bounded through the $255$ quadratic Hecke
$L$-functions of the degree-$512$ field generated over $\Q(\sqrt{241})$ by
the square roots of its $\{2,3,5\}$-units. Finite facts and numerical
inequalities are certified by exact computation and interval arithmetic.
\end{abstract}
